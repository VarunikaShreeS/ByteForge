Safety Tips

Practical checks that work whether the message arrived by SMS, WhatsApp, email or a social network.

Slow down when a message creates urgency

Deadlines, threats of disconnection and "final notice" wording exist to stop you checking. Any genuine institution will still be there after you have verified the message through a channel you chose yourself.

Never share an OTP, PIN or password

No bank, platform or brand support team needs your one-time password. If you have already shared one, contact your bank immediately and change the password on that account.

Reach the business through a channel you find yourself

Open the business's app or type their address into the browser. Do not use the number, link or QR code that was sent to you — that is exactly what the sender controls.

Check the address properly

Look at the part immediately before the first single slash and count the dots. Pay attention to lookalike characters such as 0 for o, 1 for l and rn for m. Extra words like "secure" or "verify" in the address are common.

Look up the official identity

Check the business's published official channels and compare them with what you were sent. A mismatch in the address, the handle or the payment destination is the single strongest warning sign.

Treat unusual payment requests as a stop signal

Requests for a transfer to a personal account, a UPI ID that does not match the business name, gift cards or cryptocurrency are consistently reported in scams.

A prize you did not enter is never a prize

Winning a draw you have no memory of entering, or being asked for a "processing fee", a "tax payment" or card details to release a reward, is a recognised pattern.

Report it, even if you did not lose money

A report helps other people recognise the pattern. You do not need an account, and you do not need to give your contact details.

If you have already been caught

These steps are worth taking immediately, in this order.

  1. Contact your bank or card issuer and ask them to block the transaction and the card.
  2. Change the password on the affected account, and on any account that reused it.
  3. Report the message to the platform it came through, using their in-app reporting tool.
  4. Keep the original message, the sender's details and any payment reference — do not delete them.
  5. File a report with your local cybercrime authority for your country.

SENTINEL AI cannot contact your bank or the authorities on your behalf, and does not do so automatically.
